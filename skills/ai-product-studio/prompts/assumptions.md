# ASSUMPTIONS TO TEST

Work from everything so far: the Idea recap, the AI job, and the setup you worked out with its source statuses. If Setup has not run, run it first; a source marked build is usually an assumption.

Choose silently, as a senior UX researcher would. Separate what the founder said happened from what they believe will happen, and hunt the comfortable beliefs they would nod along to. Check the nonprofit failure modes: the beneficiary, the user, and the payer are usually different people; gatekeepers veto silently; dignity and trust drive adoption more than convenience; the pilot that worked with motivated early users collapses with everyone else. Check the four forces on the user: the push away from today's way, the pull of the new one, the anxiety about switching, and the habit that holds the old way in place.

Check, too, the ways tech nonprofits quietly fail after launch, from `references/ai4h-2026-key-findings.md`: the champion who drives the work leaves; AI and running costs rise with use while funding covers only the pilot; the data that was supposed to arrive does not; nobody is paid to monitor and fix it once it is live. One of these belongs in the five when the session gives a reason to think it applies.

Pick **exactly five**. One is always the **format and access** assumption: will this user actually adopt the channel chosen in Setup row 1, made concrete (one more app to download and return to; one more tool with no home-screen icon; a thread full of other messages), with sign-in friction folded in when it bites. The other four are the beliefs most likely to sink the product if wrong, ranked by severity times how unlikely the founder is to spot it alone. An assumption that would not change what gets built first does not make the list. If a Custom-trained job was picked, at least one covers data, validation, or fairness.

The reply is only this: "Question: These are the assumptions your plan rests on. Which have you already confirmed, by seeing it happen? Reply with the numbers, or 'none.'" then five numbered lines, each one short sentence written as the founder's own belief. No tags, nothing else. Any number they do not list is an assumption to test. If they reply "idk," treat all five as untested.

**Then the Choices recap,** in the next reply, the second and last place you restate anything. At most 250 words, no lead-in sentence:

- **Setup.** The seven rows in four or five short lines, guesses marked "(guess)." One line on any two rows that do not fit together.
- **What the AI needs.** One plain line per job picked (see AI jobs). No tier names.
- **Assumptions to test.** One line each: the assumption in five words or so, then **Experiment:** one step the founder can run within two weeks, with a number and a signal ("ask five teachers to try one lesson; if three do, it holds"). Something one founder does in a conversation or by watching real use. Never "do user research." Mark any the founder confirmed "(confirmed)" with no experiment.

End with: "Question: **A** for the full PRD (about 2,000 words), **B** for a one-page brief, or tell me what to change. Guesses stay marked unconfirmed in either." Any clear yes locks the configuration.
