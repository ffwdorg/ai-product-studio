# 2026 AI for Humanity Report: key findings

Knowledge file for the AI Product Studio. Source: Bitner-Laird, L., Wrenn, J., Cavalier, T., Nelson, K., & Davenport, N. (2026). *2026 AI for Humanity Report.* Fast Forward. https://www.ffwd.org/ai-for-humanity-report. Figures below are the report's own; cite them as "the 2026 AI for Humanity Report found." Never adjust one. When a founder wants the full report, point them to https://www.ffwd.org/ai-for-humanity-report.

**Who was surveyed.** 119 AI-powered nonprofits (APNs): organizations that use AI to deliver their products, programs, or services, making AI central to their impact. Plus 20 interviews (10 APNs, 10 AI funders). 46% of respondents were Fast Forward portfolio organizations. Self-reported, not a random sample; the report says the findings do not generalize to the sector as a whole and should not be read as evidence that AI caused the reported changes. A coach that cites a number carries that caveat.

## Scale

- 59% have 10 or fewer employees.
- Just over six in ten run their AI on less than $150K a year.

## What they build

- Most common applications: research, data analysis, and knowledge retrieval (75%); directly serving the people they serve (73%); chatbots and conversational AI (71%); data extraction and document processing (54%).
- Most use AI in several ways at once rather than one tool for one task.
- They identify a specific community or organizational need first, then ask whether AI is the right tool.
- 72% build on top of existing models or frameworks; 55% customize off-the-shelf tools with their own data.

## What sinks them after launch

- 90% of those piloting or scaling have a plan to scale; only 24% have the resources to execute it.
- 45% name rising costs of AI tools and infrastructure as the top external threat.
- Philanthropy typically funds exploration and pilots, less often maintenance and scale. Post-launch costs (testing, monitoring, computing, licensing) rise with use, and few grants fund governance and monitoring.
- 64% name a champion who drives the vision as a top enabler. When that person leaves, the work is exposed.

## Risk and trust

- 88% are concerned about delivering inaccurate information; 84% about exposing or misusing personal data; 66% worry AI could erode trust with the people they serve.
- 76% collect ongoing feedback from the people they serve; 66% test their AI tools directly with them before launch.

## What the report advises nonprofits

Keep asking why AI. Name what stays human and be transparent about AI use. Budget for endurance and price the hidden labor: monitoring, testing, and iteration are where the money goes. Design feedback loops that include community voices.
