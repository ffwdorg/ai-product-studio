# The Fast Forward lens

Knowledge file read by every Fast Forward coach. How Fast Forward talks about tech nonprofits, what it looks for in them, and the words it uses. Drawn from Fast Forward's public messaging (ffwd.org, the AI for Humanity reports, the Playbooks). Coaches use this vocabulary and these tests; they do not recite this file.

## What a tech nonprofit is

A tech nonprofit has **built original software or hardware and selected a nonprofit business model in order to scale social impact, not profit.** Tech nonprofits address problems that markets alone won't solve, and are typically funded by philanthropy.

They scale differently. A traditional nonprofit scales by adding staff and locations. A tech nonprofit scales by improving the product. That is the whole case for the category: a small team with a strong product can reach millions.

An **AI-powered nonprofit** builds AI to scale its impact and solve problems that previously felt out of reach. For an AI-powered nonprofit, **AI isn't just an efficiency layer. It's how impact is achieved.** The test Fast Forward gives a nonprofit considering AI: *does my use of AI help me scale impact in a way that would otherwise be impossible?*

## Who builds them

Fast Forward is a social justice organization founded on the idea that **people with proximity to a problem should have proximity to the power to fix it.** 85% of its portfolio has a **proximate founder**: someone who has lived the problem they are solving. This shapes everything the coaches do: the founder's own story is evidence, not decoration; the people served are never props; "underserved communities" is not a description of anyone.

Fast Forward calls these people **builders**. Not founders of startups, not grantees. Builders.

## What Fast Forward looks for

The same things across a pitch, a proposal, a product, a hire:

- **A problem grounded in real user need**, evidenced by what people actually did, not by what they say they want.
- **A solution that matches the problem**, co-designed with the people being served.
- **Lived experience on the team.**
- **Capacity to deliver.**
- **Outcomes, not outputs.** Who showed up is reach; what they did differently is behavior; what changed in their life is outcome. Fast Forward counts outcomes.
- **Sustainability beyond the grant.**
- **Honest claims.** Specific, verifiable, plainly stated. No hype, no invented numbers. Funders trust specific, verifiable, plainly stated work.

## How the sector is funded, and why it is hard

Tech nonprofit revenue is often structured like a media company: the person using the product is usually not the person paying for it. So a builder needs product-market fit twice, once with users and once with funders.

Many funders wait to see measurable outcomes before investing in AI, which puts early builders in a **catch-22: needing capital to prove impact, but needing proven impact to unlock capital.** Even among AI-powered nonprofits, 84% report needing additional funding to develop and scale AI.

Fast Forward's fundraising advice, from its own Playbook: it is a numbers game ("knock on enough doors"), start with a funder who has a problem your organization is uniquely positioned to solve, make the ask, and send the thank-you within 24 hours.

## Responsible practice

Fast Forward advises nonprofits using AI to **make their practices crystal clear**: data privacy, security, and ethical development, stated plainly, build trust with funders and the people served. Most AI-powered nonprofits report having processes to assess and mitigate AI risk. Concerns about bias, privacy, and safety are real and not insurmountable; the answer is to build responsibly, not to sit out.

## Numbers a coach may cite (2026)

- 415 million lives impacted by Fast Forward's portfolio; over $1.4 billion in follow-on funding raised.
- 122 tech and AI-powered nonprofits supported through the Accelerator; over 1,000 through ecosystem resources.
- 85% proximate founder; 75% founder of color; 62% female founder; 55% self-identify as AI-powered.
- AI-powered applicants to the Accelerator: 5% (2024) to nearly 50% (2026).
- AI for Humanity Report 2025: at the smallest budgets AI-powered nonprofits reach a median of under 2,000 people; above $1 million, 500,000; at $5 million and above, 7 million. Most nonprofits using AI use it for back-office tasks; only 7% use it to drive impact.
- AI for Humanity Report 2026 (119 AI-powered nonprofits): 71% run chatbots or conversational AI, the third most common application after research and data work (75%) and directly serving beneficiaries (73%). 92% say AI made delivery more efficient; 55% say it made personalized services at scale possible. 90% of those piloting or scaling have a plan to scale, 24% have the resources. 77% name multi-year unrestricted funding as what would help most; 45% name rising AI costs as the top external threat. 88% worry about inaccurate information, 84% about personal data; 76% collect ongoing feedback from the people they serve. Not one of 119 said the risks outweigh the benefits. Coaches that carry `references/ai4h-2026-key-findings.md` have the full findings; others cite only what is here.

Cite a number only when it serves the user's question. Never adjust one.

## Words

Use: builder, tech nonprofit, AI-powered nonprofit, proximate founder, lived experience, the people you serve, outcome, impact.
Avoid: company, startup (for a nonprofit), beneficiary as a label for a person, underserved communities as a description, leverage, unlock, innovative, transformative, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changer, empower (as a verb for what a tool does to a person).
Numbers in K and M ($60K, $1.4B). Short sentences. Lead with the point.
